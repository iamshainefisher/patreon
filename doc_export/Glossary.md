# Glossary

Every term the series uses, in one standing page that each post links to. Publish it as its own Patreon post, open to all patrons, and add a row whenever a new post introduces a term.

| Term | Meaning | First used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6th chord | A major triad with the 6th note of the scale added: E6 is E–G#–B–C#. | ER2 |
| 7th chord | A triad with a 7th added on top. Emaj7 adds D#; Em7 is E–G–B–D. | ER2 |
| Add9 chord | A triad with the 2nd note of the scale added an octave up: Eadd9 is E–G#–B–F#. | ER8 |
| Aeolian | The natural minor scale as a mode: major with the 3rd, 6th and 7th lowered. | M1, M6 |
| Arpeggio | A chord played one note at a time instead of all together. | E1 |
| Barre chord | A guitar chord where one finger presses several strings across a fret. | F3 |
| Chord | Three or more notes played together. | F1 |
| Chord progression | A series of chords played in order. | F1 |
| Circle of fifths | Keys arranged a fifth apart in a loop; neighbouring keys share six of their seven notes. | F5 |
| Diminished | A minor chord with its fifth lowered by a half step; it sounds tense and unsettled. | F3 |
| Dominant 7th | A major triad with a lowered 7th added: E7 is E–G#–B–D. The sound of the blues. | ER1 |
| Dorian | Major with the 3rd and 7th lowered; a minor mode with a raised 6th. | M5 |
| Fifth | The note five scale degrees above the root; B is the fifth of E. | F1, F5 |
| Flat (♭) | Lowers a note by a half step. | F1 |
| Flat nine (♭9) | A dominant 7th chord with a lowered 2nd added on top: B7♭9 adds C. | ER3 |
| Half step | The smallest distance between notes: one fret on guitar, the next key on piano. | F1, F2 |
| Home chord | The chord built on the first note of a key or mode, where the music feels at rest. | F6 |
| ii – V – I | A three-chord move home, built on the 2nd, 5th and 1st scale degrees; the backbone of jazz. | ER2 |
| Ionian | The major scale under its modal name. | M1, M2 |
| Key | The home note and scale that a piece is built around. | F1 |
| Locrian | Major with the 2nd, 3rd, 5th, 6th and 7th lowered; its home chord is diminished. | M8 |
| Loop | A short chord sequence repeated through a whole section or song. | ER10 |
| Lowered / raised | A scale degree moved down or up by a half step, written ♭3 or #4. | M3 |
| Lydian | Major with the 4th raised. | M3 |
| Major and minor | Major chords sound bright (E–G#–B); minor chords sound darker (E–G–B). | F1, F3 |
| Major scale | Seven notes in the pattern whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half. | F2 |
| Mixolydian | Major with the 7th lowered. | M4 |
| Mode | A scale with its own pattern of whole and half steps and its own mood. | M1 |
| Nashville numbers | A way of numbering chords 1 to 7 so any key reads the same. | F4 |
| Natural minor | The minor scale with no altered notes; the same as Aeolian. | M6 |
| Octave | The same note eight scale steps higher or lower. | F2 |
| Phrygian | Major with the 2nd, 3rd, 6th and 7th lowered. | M7 |
| Power chord | A two-note chord of root and fifth, with no 3rd, written E5. Neither major nor minor. | ER9 |
| Roman numerals | Numbers that name chords by scale degree; capitals are major, lower case are minor, ° is diminished. | F3 |
| Root | The note a scale or chord is named after. | F1 |
| Scale | A set of notes, usually seven, arranged in steps up from the root. | F1 |
| Scale degree | A note's position in the scale, numbered 1 to 7. | F1, F2 |
| Sharp (#) | Raises a note by a half step. | F1 |
| Suspended chord (sus) | A chord whose 3rd is swapped for the 4th (sus4) or 2nd (sus2): Bsus4 is B–E–F#. | ER8 |
| Tab | Guitar notation showing which fret and string to play. | F1 |
| Third (in a chord) | The chord note two scale steps above the root; it decides whether a chord is major or minor. | E1 |
| Triad | A three-note chord made of the root, third and fifth. | F1, F3 |
| Twelve-bar blues | A 12-bar pattern built on the I, IV and V chords. | ER1 |
| Vamp | Two chords repeated as a groove. | ER7 |
| Whole step | Two half steps, or two frets on guitar. | F1, F2 |
